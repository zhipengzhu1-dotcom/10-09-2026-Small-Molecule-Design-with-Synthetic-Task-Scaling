# The Tier-2 docking oracle: what replaces POSIT + Chemgauss4, and what it costs here

Resolves [#4](https://github.com/zhipengzhu1-dotcom/10-09-2026-Small-Molecule-Design-with-Synthetic-Task-Scaling/issues/4).
Measured 2026-10-11 on the machine described in
[`rtx5090-wsl2-grpo-bringup.md`](rtx5090-wsl2-grpo-bringup.md) (RTX 5090, 24 logical
cores, 31 GiB RAM, WSL2 / Ubuntu 24.04).

Prototype code — the primary source behind every number below — is on the throwaway branch
[`prototype/docking-oracle-4`](https://github.com/zhipengzhu1-dotcom/10-09-2026-Small-Molecule-Design-with-Synthetic-Task-Scaling/tree/prototype/docking-oracle-4/prototypes/docking-oracle).
It is never merged.

Everything in the tables is **measured on this hardware** unless marked *inferred*.

## The decision

**smina with the Vinardo scoring function, in `--minimize` mode, on a pose produced by
RDKit `ConstrainedEmbed` against the lead's crystallographic coordinates, with an MCS
template fallback.** CPU only. No GPU docking engine.

Measured cost: **0.133 s per molecule** end-to-end, median, cold process, single core.

That is roughly **1,000x cheaper than the 126 s/complex figure** that disqualified CPU
Vina/smina for this project — because that figure is full-exhaustiveness *global* docking,
and the paper's Tier-2 is *template* docking. Local minimisation from a known pose is a
different cost class, and the research doc's estimate of 0.1–2 s/molecule was right at its
optimistic end.

**The oracle is not the bottleneck. Generation still is**, exactly as #2 found.

## Setup

Four protein–ligand complexes from RCSB, chosen for a lead-optimisation-shaped size
spread, **not** from PLINDER: [#6](https://github.com/zhipengzhu1-dotcom/10-09-2026-Small-Molecule-Design-with-Synthetic-Task-Scaling/issues/6)
owns PLINDER subsetting, and cost-per-call barely depends on which real pocket is used.
Re-validating on real PLINDER systems is left to #6; the cost figures are not expected to move.

| PDB | ligand | heavy atoms | rot. bonds | receptor atoms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1IEP | STI (imatinib) | 37 | 7 | 4,458 |
| 1M17 | AQ4 (erlotinib) | 29 | 10 | 2,497 |
| 3ERT | OHT (4-hydroxytamoxifen) | 29 | 8 | 1,932 |
| 1HVR | XK2 | 46 | 8 | 1,826 |

120 analogs (30 per target), generated by decorating open aromatic CH positions with a
small substituent library — a scaffold-preserving lead-optimisation move, so the crystal
ligand is always a clean template. **120/120 scored successfully.**

A fifth target, 3PTB (trypsin/benzamidine), was dropped: CCD bond-order assignment failed
(`No matching found`). See *Failure contract* — this is a curation-time failure mode, and
one of the five complexes tried hit it.

## Per-call cost

120 molecules, one cold smina process each, single core:

| stage | median | mean | p90 | p99 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ConstrainedEmbed` | 0.059 s | 0.082 s | 0.144 s | 0.330 s | 0.332 s |
| `smina --minimize` | 0.052 s | 0.063 s | 0.113 s | 0.121 s | 0.122 s |
| **end-to-end** | **0.133 s** | 0.145 s | 0.211 s | 0.369 s | 0.370 s |

Scores: median -10.67 kcal/mol, range -15.15 to -5.46. The 1IEP crystal ligand itself
scores -13.63 (`--score_only`) / -14.58 (`--minimize`), so the analogs sit sensibly below
their own lead.

**Pose generation, not scoring, is the expensive half.** smina's internally reported
`Refine time` is **0.011 s**; the rest of its 0.052 s is process startup and receptor
parsing. This is the most useful fact here, because all of it is amortisable.

## Throughput: three ways to spend the cost

Same 120 poses, pre-embedded so docking is timed alone:

| path | ms/molecule | note |
|---|---|---|
| one process per molecule, sequential | 62.1 | startup-dominated |
| **batched per target (multi-model SDF), `--cpu 1`** | **10.0** | **6.2x from amortising startup + receptor parse** |
| batched per target, `--cpu 8` | 9.9 | smina's threading buys nothing for minimisation |
| per molecule, 4 processes | 15.7 | 64 mol/s |
| per molecule, 12 processes | 6.4 | 157 mol/s |
| per molecule, 24 processes | 4.6 | 219 mol/s |

And the other half, `ConstrainedEmbed`:

| processes | ms/molecule | throughput |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 80.9 | 12.4 mol/s |
| 12 | 8.3 | 121 mol/s |
| 24 | 5.5 | 182 mol/s |

Two consequences for the spec:

1. **`--cpu` is the wrong knob.** smina's own threading does nothing for local
   minimisation; process-level parallelism is what scales (62.1 to 4.6 ms/mol).
2. **Parallelising embedding matters more than parallelising docking**, since embedding is
   the slower half single-threaded (80.9 vs 62.1 ms/mol).

## Does it fit inside the RL loop?

Using #2's measured step cost of **39.0 s at `num_generations=8`**, and that run's observed
~5 turns per episode, with one scored molecule per turn, gives **40 oracle calls per step**.

| rollout style | oracle per step | share of step |
|---|---|---|
| batch-of-one, as #2 measured today | 40 x 0.133 s = **5.3 s** | **~12%** |
| group stepped in lockstep, embed 24-way + dock batched | 40 x ~0.016 s = **0.6 s** | **~1.6%** |

*Inferred* from those rates, for an illustrative 300-step arm — **#11 owns the real step
count**:

- docking: **27 min** unbatched, **3 min** batched
- generation: **3.25 h**
- 1,000 scores: **2.2 min** unbatched, against the **35 h** that disqualified
  full-exhaustiveness Vina

Either way the oracle is comfortably affordable, and it stays affordable even if the policy
proposes several molecules per turn. It only becomes a concern if a single turn scores tens
of molecules, which is itself batchable.

## No GPU docking engine

Uni-Dock and the Vina-GPU family were **not** installed, for two reasons recorded here so
the choice can be overridden on evidence rather than re-litigated:

1. **Wrong workload.** Both are global-search batch screeners, built to amortise a GPU over
   huge libraries. The paper's Tier-2 is template-constrained local refinement, which the
   measurements above put at 10 ms/molecule on a CPU core. There is no throughput problem
   left for a GPU to solve.
2. **The VRAM is already spoken for, and contingently so.** #2 measured the training
   process at a flat **18.6–20.4 GiB** of a 31.84 GiB card, with Unsloth pinning
   `gpu_memory_utilization` to 47.13% of *total*. Headroom is therefore **~1 GiB with a busy
   Windows desktop and ~9 GiB with a quiet one** — it depends on what the desktop is doing,
   which is not something to bet a multi-hour run on. Neither Uni-Dock nor Vina-GPU
   documents a minimum VRAM figure.

Should GPU docking ever be wanted, `gpu_memory_utilization` is explicitly pinnable (#2's
recommendation to #11), so VRAM could be budgeted deliberately — but nothing here needs it.

## gnina, for #9

gnina v1.3.3 runs on this machine. Three practical notes:

- The release asset is **`gnina.cuda12.8.static`** (~2 GB), not `gnina`.
- It needs `libcudnn.so.9`, which **torch's own wheels already ship**. Point
  `LD_LIBRARY_PATH` at `$VENV/lib/python3.12/site-packages/nvidia/*/lib` and it runs with
  nothing installed and no `sudo`.
- `--cnn default` is **not** a valid model name (`Usage error: Invalid model name`).

Timed on 30 pre-embedded 1IEP poses:

| configuration | ms/molecule |
|---|---|
| `--cnn fast --cnn_scoring rescore`, GPU | 113.9 |
| `--cnn fast --cnn_scoring rescore`, `--no_gpu` | **48.6** |
| smina/Vinardo, batched, for reference | 11.6 |

**gnina is faster on CPU than GPU at this batch size** — GPU initialisation dominates 30
molecules — and it used no measurable VRAM. So gnina is available to #9 as an expensive
held-out oracle at ~4–10x smina's cost, with **no VRAM conflict with vLLM at all**. That is
a better position than the research doc's 16–23 s/complex estimate implied, because again
that figure was full docking, not rescoring a supplied pose.

## The template-match deviation — the finding that matters most

Requiring the whole lead as a substructure makes **any scaffold-modifying proposal
unscorable**. POSIT would still place such a molecule. Left alone, this silently imposes a
hard scaffold constraint during training — which the paper explicitly does *not* have: its
`contains_scaffold` is **evaluation-only by design**, because exposing it during training
caused mode collapse.

An unscorable proposal earns `r_invalid`, so the policy would be penalised for scaffold
hops it was never told were forbidden. That is a reward-shaping bug, not a cosmetic one.

**Fix, measured: fall back to the maximum common substructure.** Embed against the MCS of
proposal and lead, keeping the lead's crystallographic coordinates for the shared part.

| proposal | strict template | MCS fallback | MCS core | MCS cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| strict analog (added methyl) | OK | OK | 37 atoms | 1.5 ms |
| truncated (tail dropped) | **FAIL** | OK | 29 atoms | 0.4 ms |
| different chemotype | **FAIL** | OK | 15 atoms | 0.2 ms |
| off-scaffold entirely (`CCO`) | FAIL | **FAIL** *(correctly)* | 1 atom | 0.1 ms |

3/4 scorable against 1/4, at a median **0.3 ms** — free. And `CCO` still fails, so the
fallback widens coverage to genuine analogs without scoring unrelated molecules.

`min_core_atoms` (8 here) is therefore **a spec parameter, not an implementation detail**:
it sets how far from the lead a proposal may wander and still be scored. Too high and the
implicit scaffold constraint returns; too low and nonsense earns a real-looking score.

## Failure contract

The oracle returns `(score, reason)` and **never a garbage number**. Verified by execution:

| input | result |
|---|---|
| invalid SMILES (`C1CC1C(((C`) | `parse_failed` |
| empty string | `no_template_match` *(RDKit parses `""` into an empty mol, so it slips past the parse check and fails one stage later — still clean, just differently labelled)* |
| valid but off-scaffold (`CCO`) | `no_template_match` / `mcs_too_small(1)` |
| MCS below threshold | `mcs_too_small(n)` |
| garbage SDF | `no_score_parsed` |
| missing SDF | `smina_exit_1` |
| CCD bond-order mismatch (3PTB) | curation-time failure, target dropped |

Every one of these maps to the paper's `r_invalid`. The curation-time failure is different
in kind: it kills a *target*, not a proposal, so it must be caught when the system set is
built rather than inside the reward path.

**PoseBusters: dataset curation only, not the reward path.** *Inferred, not measured* — the
reward path already rejects unscorable molecules cheaply, and smina `--minimize` starts from
a tethered crystallographic pose, so grossly non-physical geometry is not the failure mode
here. Running PoseBusters per reward call would add an unmeasured cost to the one path that
must stay cheap. This is a recommendation to #11, not a closed decision.

## Sign convention

**More negative = tighter binding**, in kcal/mol — the same direction as Chemgauss4, so the
paper's reward equations carry over with no sign flip. Vinardo values here run about -5 to
-15. This is *not* numerically comparable to Chemgauss4; only the direction and the rough
scale transfer, which is all Tier-2 needs.

## Incidental findings worth keeping

- **RDKit coexists cleanly with the pinned GRPO stack.** `uv pip install rdkit` into
  `~/.venvs/stsc` resolves to adding **exactly one package** (`rdkit==2026.9.1`), touching
  no pin and leaving `numpy 2.2.6` alone. The oracle can live in the training process. Dry
  run only; nothing was installed into `stsc`.
- **The smina static binary from SourceForge is `Oct 15 2019`, based on Vina 1.1.2** — older
  than conda-forge's 2020.12.10, but it accepts `--scoring vinardo`, `--minimize`,
  `--score_only`, `--local_only`, `--autobox_ligand` and `--autobox_add`. One 9.9 MB
  download, no package manager, no `sudo`.
- **`--quiet` suppresses the `Affinity:` line itself**, so a scoring wrapper that passes it
  parses nothing and every molecule looks like a failure. Do not re-pay this one.
- Receptor prep needed no PDBQT conversion: smina reads `.pdb` directly.
- **Licensing**: smina's own licence is ambiguous (the tree ships both Apache-2.0 and
  GPLv2 texts), but this project only *invokes* it as a subprocess and redistributes
  nothing, so no copyleft obligation attaches to this repo. gnina is Apache-2.0.

## What this deliberately did not settle

- **Real PLINDER systems.** Deferred to #6 by design. Cost-per-call is not expected to
  move; pocket-specific score *quality* was not assessed at all.
- **Score quality.** Nothing here checks whether Vinardo ranks analogs *correctly*. These
  are cost, failure-mode and plumbing measurements only. The research doc's reward-hacking
  warning — that a learned CNN score is more gameable than a physics term — stands as the
  reason to keep Vinardo primary and gnina held out.
- **Protonation.** Receptors were used as deposited, with no Dimorphite-DL or pH treatment.
  Fine for timing; a fidelity gap the spec must note.
- **Batched-across-group scoring was measured but not built.** The 1.6%-of-step figure
  assumes the lockstep rollout change #11 is already considering; today's batch-of-one path
  gives ~12%, which is affordable anyway.
